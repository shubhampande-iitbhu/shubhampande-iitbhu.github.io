My research focuses on developing energy-efficient compute substrates through innovations spanning emerging memory technologies, novel devices, circuit prototyping, and new compute paradigms. The goal is to integrate ideas across the computing stack—including materials, hardware, and algorithms—to surpass the performance limitations of conventional computing. In line with this theme, my research activities are concentrated on exploring the following areas.

---

## Emerging Memories

My research addresses memory technologies for next‑generation computing. With CMOS scaling slowing while AI‑driven compute demand rises, emerging memories such as resistive random‑access memory (ReRAM) and ferroelectric field‑effect transistors (FeFETs) are promising candidates for both storage and in‑memory computing. Device optimization for storage and compute often imposes conflicting requirements; my work develops a fundamental understanding of the physical mechanisms that determine memory characteristics, quantifies trade‑offs, and produces predictive device and circuit models. I also investigate reliability and variability, and derive practical design guidelines tailored to target applications. In addition to emerging memories, I advance state‑of‑the‑art technologies such as DRAM to improve their performance and suitability for AI workloads — specifically DRAM cell, sense‑amplifier, and peripheral design for improved speed, density, and refresh‑power trade‑offs.

## Emerging Computing Paradigms

This research thrust focuses on developing computing paradigms that harness the inherent physical laws of device physics and circuit interconnections to enable energy-efficient computing. Work in this area spans cross-layer optimization ranging from tailoring device properties to target applications, circuit prototyping, and application-level modeling and analysis. Notable research directions I have pursued include:

1) developing neo-Hebbian synapses for the online training of neuromorphic hardware,
2) ReRAM-enabled kernels for reservoir computing that provide post-fabrication tunability, and
3) advanced CMOS image sensors using ReRAM to overcome the fundamental trade-off between dynamic range and conversion gain.
